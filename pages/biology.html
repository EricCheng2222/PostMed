Biology

Half of the 高醫 後醫 science paper is general biology — 45 questions, 75 marks, sat in English alongside the biochemistry half. It is not the molecular-biology paper most candidates expect. It is broad Campbell organismal biology, and the single most under-weighted block in typical study plans is plants.

3 of 14 notes published Weighted by 4 years of past papers KMU Post-Bacc Medicine · Informatics Track

What the paper actually asks

The ideaBefore you read a single note, know where the marks are. The biology half is not weighted the way a textbook is ordered, and it is not weighted the way most people revise. Four years of past papers say plants and animal physiology are jointly worth almost a third of it, while molecular genetics — the part everyone over-prepares — is under a tenth.

The format

One paper, 「普通生物及生化概論」, 150 of 400 written marks. 90 single-choice questions with five options, 100 minutes, entirely in English. Q1–15 and Q31–60 are biology; the rest is biochemistry. About 66 seconds per question.

The marking

Wrong answers are penalised: −0.25 in Part I, −0.5 in Part II, floored at zero per section. Blanks score nothing. With five options a blind guess is exactly break-even — so the moment you eliminate one option, answering beats leaving it blank.

The source

Candidates work from Campbell Biology in English. Most years carry a few items beyond the textbook, clustering in cell biology, molecular biology, evolution and ecology.

The trap that costs the most marks

Skipping plants, diversity and ecology loses you roughly 40 % of the biology half. Plant questions have never dropped below seven in any year examined. A second, subtler trap: distractors are sometimes lifted verbatim as sentences from textbook prose, so an option can sound exactly like something you read and still be wrong. These notes teach the reasoning that lets you reject a plausible-sounding sentence, not just the headline fact.

Cells, organelles, membranes and transport

The eukaryotic cell as a set of compartments that each solve one problem; the endomembrane system as a production line; mitochondria and chloroplasts and the endosymbiotic evidence; the cytoskeleton and motor proteins; membrane structure and the whole transport hierarchy from simple diffusion to primary active transport; the cell-surface junctions.

The cell cycle, mitosis, meiosis and cancer

Interphase and the checkpoints that gate it; mitosis stage by stage; meiosis and the two events that generate variation, with the mitosis/meiosis contrasts examiners reuse; non-disjunction and the aneuploidies; cyclins and CDKs; proto-oncogenes and tumour suppressors, and why cancer needs several hits.

03BIOLOGY

Classical and chromosomal genetics

Mendel's laws and what they actually claim; monohybrid and dihybrid crosses; the deviations — incomplete and co-dominance, multiple alleles, epistasis, pleiotropy, polygenic traits; sex linkage and pedigree analysis; linkage, recombination frequency and map units; the chromosomal disorders.

04BIOLOGY

Molecular genetics and the control of gene expression

The biology half's own molecular block — overlapping biochemistry note 05 but examined differently. Operons and prokaryotic control; eukaryotic regulation from chromatin to transcription factors to RNA processing; epigenetics; the regulatory RNAs; mutation types and their consequences; the standard laboratory toolkit.

05BIOLOGY

Evolution and population genetics

Natural selection stated precisely, and the evidence for common descent; Hardy–Weinberg as a null model and the calculations built on it; the five forces that break it; modes of selection; speciation and reproductive isolation; molecular clocks and neutral theory; the misconceptions examiners test.

Biodiversity, phylogeny and classification

The three domains and why the tree was redrawn; reading a phylogenetic tree properly — clades, monophyly, homology versus analogy; the major animal phyla and the body-plan characters that separate them; the land-plant lineages; systematics and the naming rules.

Prokaryotes, viruses, protists and fungi

Exactly four questions every single year examined — the most predictable block on the paper. Bacterial structure, Gram staining, metabolism and genetic exchange; archaea; viral structure and the lytic and lysogenic cycles, retroviruses; protist diversity and the medically important parasites; fungal groups, life cycles and their ecological role.
