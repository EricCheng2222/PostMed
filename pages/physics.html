Physics

A complete pass through first-year university physics, in fifteen notes. Freshman physics is not fifteen unrelated topics — it is a handful of conservation laws applied over and over to different objects. Energy, momentum and charge are conserved; forces cause acceleration; fields fall off with distance. Everything below is those ideas, wearing different clothes.

15 notes · complete 150 self-test questions KMU Post-Bacc Medicine · Informatics Track ~10 h of reading

How these notes are built

① Definition and formula, at the top

Every section opens with a panel giving the definition, the governing formula, the condition under which it is valid, and what every symbol means in SI units. You can revise from those panels alone the night before.

② Then the reasoning

Underneath, the explanation of why the formula has that shape, the worked examples, the diagrams, and the sign conventions and confusions that actually cost marks.

③ Then ten questions

Each note ends with a self-test that marks instantly and explains every answer — including why the tempting wrong option is wrong.

If you are short on time

Notes 01–05 are load-bearing: almost everything later reuses free-body diagrams, energy conservation and momentum conservation. If you only have a week, read those properly, then take the rest from their formula sheets and self-tests.

Momentum, impulse and collisions

Impulse as the area under a force–time curve, and why stretching a collision in time is what airbags and crumple zones are for. notes 06–08
06PHYSICS

Gravitation and orbital motion

The inverse-square law and the shell theorem; how g varies with altitude and depth; why gravitational potential energy is negative and goes as 1/r; escape velocity; circular orbits and the geostationary case worked numerically; Kepler's three laws derived rather than recited; and why astronauts are weightless without being beyond gravity.

Orbit energy diagram 10 questions
07PHYSICS

Oscillations and simple harmonic motion

One condition — acceleration proportional to displacement and directed back towards it — generates the whole chapter. Displacement, velocity and acceleration and their phase relationships; the spring and pendulum periods and what they are independent of; energy in SHM; damping and the three regimes; driven oscillation and resonance.

Waves and sound

What actually travels in a wave, and why crossing a boundary changes the wavelength but never the frequency. Superposition and interference, standing waves and the harmonic series for strings and for open and closed pipes, the decibel scale, beats, and the Doppler effect with a sign convention you can trust — plus its use in medical ultrasound.

09PHYSICS

Fluid statics and dynamics

The most clinically loaded chapter in the course. Hydrostatic pressure and why blood pressure is quoted in mmHg; Archimedes; continuity and Bernoulli and what they really assume; viscosity and Poiseuille's fourth-power law, which is why a small change in vessel radius changes everything; the Reynolds number, bruits and murmurs; and Laplace's law applied to the alveolus and to surfactant.

10PHYSICS

Thermal physics and thermodynamics

The difference between heat, internal energy and temperature, made precise. Specific and latent heat and why water's values matter for thermoregulation; conduction, convection and radiation; the ideal gas law and the kinetic theory behind it; the first law applied to the four standard processes; entropy, heat engines and the Carnot limit — and why a living organism does not violate the second law.

11PHYSICS

Electric charge, field and potential

Coulomb's law and the field it creates; Gauss's law and the symmetries that make it useful; conductors in equilibrium and electrostatic shielding; potential as a scalar, which is the single most useful contrast with the field; equipotentials and the field as a gradient; capacitance, dielectrics and stored energy — and the cell membrane as a capacitor.

12PHYSICS

Direct-current circuits

Why a lamp lights instantly although electrons drift at a fraction of a millimetre per second. Resistance and resistivity, Ohm's law as a material property rather than a universal law, EMF and internal resistance, series and parallel reasoned out rather than recited, Kirchhoff's rules with a sign procedure that works, RC charging and the time constant, and electrical safety in a clinical setting.

13PHYSICS

Magnetism and electromagnetic induction

The magnetic force is always perpendicular to velocity, so it does no work — nearly every magnetism question is a corollary. Circular motion in a field, mass spectrometers, forces on wires and torque on loops, the fields currents produce, then Faraday and Lenz with the direction worked out explicitly, inductance, transformers, and the physics behind MRI.
